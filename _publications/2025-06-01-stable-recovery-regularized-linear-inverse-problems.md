We study when a low-complexity signal (sparse, group-sparse, or with sparse gradient) can be recovered *stably* from noisy linear measurements by convex regularization: the regularized solutions stay within a distance of the true signal proportional to the noise level. We give a characterization of stable recovery in finite dimensions that uses nonsmooth second-order information of the regularizer, and we show that for piecewise linear-quadratic regularizers stable recovery is equivalent to unique recovery. The theory yields new sufficient conditions for analysis group sparsity, including group Lasso and isotropic total variation, which we test numerically.
